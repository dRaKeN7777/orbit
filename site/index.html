The problem

Presales breaks when the deal lives in one person's inbox.

The estimate sits in a spreadsheet. The scope sits in the proposal. The delivery assumptions sit in someone's head until mobilisation. Each artefact is defensible on its own, and the deal still cannot be reconciled.

The number can't be reproduced

Ask two people how a margin was reached and you get two answers, three weeks after submission.

Approvals vanish into threads

The decision exists. The reasoning, the revision and the owner do not.

Handover leaks the margin

Delivery rebuilds the plan from the proposal rather than from what was actually costed.

How it works

Four steps, the same every time.

The console follows the shape of a security deal. It does not ask your team to work in a way they would not recognise.

  1. 01

    Capture the deal

    Log the client, the opportunity and the security scope you are being asked to cover — cloud, identity, network, data or GRC.

  2. 02

    Cost it properly

    Build the estimate from products, effort and partner rates, so the number you quote is a number you can actually deliver against.

  3. 03

    Assemble the bid

    Generate the proposal from approved content, then review it as one document instead of six attachments and a spreadsheet.

  4. 04

    Approve, hand over, renew

    Route it for approval, hand over to delivery with everything attached, and track the renewal clock from day one.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Is this just another CRM?

No. A CRM tracks the opportunity. CyberPresalesOS also holds the catalogue, the estimate and the proposal the opportunity turns into — so the number you quote and the document you send both come from the same model.

Do we have to replace our CRM?

No. If your CRM is the system of record for pipeline, keep it that way. The console is where the presales work happens once a deal is real enough to cost and write up.

How long does setup take?

A pilot starts with one team and one live deal: your product and partner catalogue, your templates, your approval route. Most teams are running a real bid through it inside the first week.

Where does our client data live?

In your deployment, behind your own sign-in, with role-based access for the people on the deal. Nothing is pooled with other customers.

Who can approve a bid?

Whoever you nominate. Approvals are routed to named approvers and the decision is recorded against the deal, so the next person can see why it was signed off.
